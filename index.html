Overview

Written by me, not a template

I started in machine learning before I understood the hardware underneath it. At 16 I was fine-tuning CNNs on histopathology slides for the Burkitt's Lymphoma Fund for Africa, working alongside people at UW, Fred Hutch, and Phenopath to build a screening tool that could run where a pathologist wasn't available. That work became a first-author paper in Frontiers in Medicine.

What pulled me toward ECE was the question of what actually happens after you call the model. A network is a few million multiply-accumulates; something physical has to schedule them, feed them, and finish in time. So I went down a layer. Now I quantize CNNs to int8 and write the SystemVerilog inference pipeline that runs them, build pipelined ARM CPUs gate by gate, and route impedance-controlled boards in Altium.

The through-line is the same: I like problems where the model and the machine have to agree with each other.
